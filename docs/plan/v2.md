# v2 — The home you can question, cost and agree on

**Goal: the assistant designs like a designer, explains itself, and hands over something worth paying for.**

The single future product plan. v1 shipped: a homeowner and a designer choose a home, generate a design with Gemini, refine it together in a shared room, accept a version and download a draft agreement. This says what comes next, and why.

Written at product level. The deep technical spec that used to live in `v2.md` and `v3.md` — the nine-role crew, A2A and IAM detail, evaluation gates, multiple floors, rate-card thinking — is preserved unchanged in [v2.backup.md](v2.backup.md) as a reference, not a commitment.

Pascal (`@pascal-app/*`) is our own engine. We can extend it, vendor its assets, and fix things upstream instead of working around them.

Related: [release map](README.md) · [v1](v1.md) · [controls](controls.md) · [archived detail](v2.backup.md)

## What is actually wrong today

A review of the shipped v1 found the safety layer is genuinely strong — immutable revisions, frozen-scope validation, atomic rejection, honest failure. The design *output* is weak, and not because the model is weak. The host caps it:

- **Everything the assistant places faces the same direction.** It cannot orient anything it puts down.
- **Furniture never touches a wall.** Placement candidates sit on a fixed interior grid, so rooms read as scattered rather than designed.
- **Nothing can sit on anything.** No lamp on a side table, no books on the bookcase, no kettle on the counter.
- **The assistant designs blind.** It has never seen the room it is designing — it works from JSON alone.
- **Sixteen pieces of furniture.** No fridge, no wardrobe, no rug, no television, no sink.
- **It cannot ask, explain, or answer.** It guesses, acts, and reports what it did.
- **VibeEstimate has no estimate.** The draft agreement is prose with no quantities and no sense of cost.

Almost all of it is small to fix. That is the point of this plan.

## What v2 delivers

1. **An assistant that places things like a designer** — against walls, facing the right way, on top of surfaces.
2. **A home worth furnishing** — a real catalog instead of sixteen items.
3. **An assistant that can see** — it looks at the plan before it designs.
4. **A conversation anchored to a place** — comment on *that wall*, not into a transcript.
5. **A real estimate** — measured quantities, and an honest view of what work like this usually costs.
6. **A crew instead of one prompt** — specialised agents with skills and tools.

Phases are ordered by value per unit of effort. Each one leaves a shippable product.

## Phase 1 — Place things like a designer

The highest value per line of code in the plan, and it changes no safety rule.

- Placement candidates carry a **rotation**, and include **wall-anchored and corner positions**, not only interior grid points. A sofa can finally have its back to a wall.
- The assistant may **ask a question back** instead of guessing — *"facing the window, or the television wall?"*
- Each change carries a short **reason**, so the homeowner reads a design decision rather than a coordinate.
- **Prompt suggestions follow the selection**: an empty room offers furnishing, a wall face offers a finish, a lamp offers light.
- Home gallery thumbnails are **rendered from the actual layout**, instead of hand-made images that can silently drift from what they depict.

**Done when** a generated living room reads as deliberate in a screenshot, with nobody editing it by hand.

## Phase 2 — A home worth furnishing

Pascal already ships a large catalog — furniture, appliances, kitchen, bathroom, outdoor — each with a model, thumbnail, floor-plan symbol, tags and measured dimensions. It is ours.

- **Bring it into the admitted catalog**: mirrored to our own storage, hashed, licensed, served same-origin. The no-arbitrary-asset-URL rule holds exactly as written.
- **Things can sit on things.** Pascal already knows how to rest an object on a floor or on another surface; the app currently forbids it outright. Lamps on tables, books on shelves, a plant on a sideboard.
- Real 2D symbols in the plan, replacing hand-drawn shapes.

**Done when** the assistant can furnish a kitchen and a bathroom convincingly, and a room contains something standing on something else.

## Phase 3 — Give the assistant eyes

- The server renders an **annotated floor plan** for the model: room names and areas, door swings, orientation, and a numbered pin at every placement it is allowed to choose.
- The assistant sees *where* each option is before choosing one, instead of picking from an abstract list.
- Because the picture now carries position, the written candidate list shrinks to bare identifiers — likely cheaper overall, not more expensive.

**Done when** side-by-side generations show the assistant choosing wall-adjacent placements without being told to.

## Phase 4 — A conversation about a place

Today a design room is a flat chat. The 3D view already resolves a click to an exact wall face, and the message request already carries that selection — then throws it away.

- **Anchored comments.** Click a wall, a floor, a chair; leave a comment pinned to it. It shows as a pin in 3D and in plan, and the chip re-selects it.
- **Change Cards.** The flat change list becomes a typed card stream: a change with its reason, a question waiting for an answer, an anchored comment, a cost card, a quantity schedule, an answer with its sources. Agents return data and admitted identifiers only — never markup.
- **Ask about this design.** A question that returns an answer and never an edit — *"is there enough light by the desk?"*, *"does this work for a family of four?"*. Nothing can change, so there is nothing to validate.

**Done when** the designer and homeowner can disagree about one specific wall, and both know which wall.

## Phase 5 — The estimate

The name of the product, finally delivered.

- **Measured quantities**, taken from geometry rather than inference: floor area per room, wall area to finish, perimeter, ceiling area, item counts. Deterministic, so they are simply true.
- **"What people usually charge."** On request, research looks up what work like this typically costs and returns an indicative range with its sources and the date it looked. Clearly not a quote, clearly not agreed.
- **The Report.** The same cards, on a button, rendered as one document the designer can hand over: the design, the changes and why, the quantities, the indicative costs, the open questions.

**Guardrail, unchanged:** the draft agreement carries no price until a human types one. A researched range is market information, not a commitment.

**Done when** a designer can hand a homeowner a report that is useful without a single invented number.

## Phase 6 — The crew

Gated on the **ADK compatibility spike** v1 already required: structured tools, bounded handoff, cancellation, authenticated calls and a pinned runtime, proven in an isolated worker before anything depends on it. If the spike fails, we have lost a spike and not a product.

A small crew, not nine roles:

| Role | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Surveyor** | Reads the annotated plan and the brief. Produces design intent — which rooms, what mood, what is missing. Proposes nothing. |
| **Creator** | Turns intent into actual changes, using tools to find candidates and catalog items. |
| **Presenter** | Turns a change set into Change Cards and the Report. What people read instead of a transcript. |
| **Researcher** | Looks things up — costs, process, materials. Always returns sources. |

### Where Skills belong

Skills carry **specialised design knowledge that does not belong in a prompt**, loaded only when an agent reaches for them:

- *Furnishing a room* — circulation, clearances, focal points, how a room is actually laid out.
- *Lighting a home* — ambient, task and accent layers, warmth, what an evening should feel like.
- *Presenting changes* — how a change card is composed, what a before-and-after must show, what may never be claimed.
- *Drafting an agreement* — what belongs in a draft, and what must stay unresolved.

### Where MCP tools belong

Tools replace **stuffing the prompt with data the agent may not need**. This is the real simplification. Today the host precomputes every possible placement and sends them all; with tools, the Creator asks a question and gets a short answer.

- **Catalog** — search admitted items by room, tag or size. Never returns a price.
- **Geometry** — *"where can a sofa go against the north wall of this room?"* Returns a few validated candidates instead of hundreds.
- **Quantities** — measured areas and counts for a room or a whole home.
- **Research** — web search, returning sources with everything.

Every toolset is allowlisted per agent. Tool results are evidence, never instructions. Scene changes still pass the same validator they pass today.

## Phase 7 — Living in it

Real polish, cheap because Pascal already has all of it and we already ship it:

- **Walk through the home** in first person, instead of orbiting it.
- **Doors and windows that open.**
- **Measurements on the plan**, shown when they are wanted.
- Compressed models, so a bigger catalog does not cost load time.

## What we still will not claim

Unchanged from v1, and load-bearing:

- No prices, signatures, approvals or permission to start work are created by anything here. A researched range is market information; an agreement price is a human decision.
- Template measurements and ceiling heights are stated assumptions, not a survey.
- Lighting is illustrative, not engineering.
- Generated prose is untrusted text. The factual change list comes from what was actually applied.
- The assistant cannot change architecture, cross a selection boundary, or touch a locked object.

## Open decisions

Deliberately unresolved, to be settled against real output rather than on paper:

- **How the crew lands** beside the working single-prompt path — behind a flag, or replacing it once proven. Default: leave production untouched until the crew earns the switch.
- **The exact cost card** — full breakdown, a single range, or one summary for the whole design.
- **Whether an indicative cost may ever enter the draft agreement.** Default is no.
- **Gemini search grounding** instead of a search library. Later, not now.
- **More ideas during implementation.** Several features here are worth reshaping once we see how they actually feel.
